Reasoning: The picture is f(y, x) = network(y, x). Shifting it right by 4 pixels means the new picture is g(y, x) = f(y, x - d), where one pixel is 2/27 in coordinate units, so d = 4 * 2/27 = 8/27 ≈ 0.296296 (no vertical shift, so y is untouched). The first layer computes sin(y*A1[0,j] + x*A1[1,j] + c1[j]). Substituting x → x - d gives sin(y*A1[0,j] + x*A1[1,j] + (c1[j] - d*A1[1,j])). So the whole transformation is absorbed exactly by the first-layer biases: c1[j] ← c1[j] - 0.296296 * A1[1,j] for every unit j. A1, A2, c2, A3 and c3 stay unchanged, since layers 2 and 3 only see h1, which is now the shifted version of the old h1. Columns that move out of the frame on the right are simply no longer sampled. The 4 uncovered columns on the left (x < -0.704) are evaluated at x - d < -1, i.e. just outside the training domain; a SIREN extrapolates smoothly there, and the left border of such a 28x28 picture is normally black, so I leave that region to the network's extrapolation rather than risk breaking other units.
