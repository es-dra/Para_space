Reasoning: A 3x3 max (dilation) is a non-linear, spatially non-local operation, and a SIREN's weights give no direct handle on it: shifting the input (adding A1·d to c1) moves the picture rather than taking a max over shifted copies, and there are no spare hidden units to build and combine shifted copies. What I can do exactly in weight space is an affine map of the output grey value, v' = a·v + b, applied before the clip to [0, 1]. Because the SIREN's output is smooth, the pixels that dilation turns bright are precisely the stroke-edge and stroke-adjacent pixels whose current values are intermediate or small-positive (roughly 0.1-0.7); a gain a > 1 pushes those to 1 (clipped), which widens every bright stroke by about a pixel on each side, while the dark background (v near 0) stays near 0 under a pure gain. A small positive offset b lifts the faint halo just outside the strokes a little further toward bright, at the cost of a slight grey lift of the background. The output layer is linear, so this is implemented entirely by scaling A3 and resetting c3 (c3' = a·c3 + b); A1, c1, A2, c2 are untouched, so the geometry of the digit is unchanged. With a = 3, b = 0.04: c3' = 3 × 0.0936 + 0.04 = 0.3208.

```edits
A3[:, 0] *= 3            # output gain 3: intermediate stroke-edge values saturate to white, strokes thicken
c3[0] = 0.3208           # 3 * 0.0936 + 0.04: keep the gain-consistent bias plus a small lift of the stroke halo
```

The edits apply v' = clip(3·v + 0.04). Only the output layer changes, so the picture's shape and position stay the same. Grey values at and just outside the stroke boundary, which the SIREN renders as partial brightness, are driven to 1, so every bright stroke grows outward by roughly one pixel, approximating the 3x3 maximum. Far background stays near black apart from a 0.04 lift and amplified faint ripples, which is the main residual error.
